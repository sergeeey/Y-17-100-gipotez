## EstimandOps L0

**Question type:** Descriptive. "For SPD matrices A of small dimension and restart lengths
s, do the two normalized-residual subsequences {y_2k}, {y_2k+1} of the restarted s-step CG
iteration (Forsythe 1968, eq. 4-5 of arXiv:2602.05394 Section 2.7) converge to single
limiting directions, tested against both randomly-drawn and adversarially-constructed SPD
matrices?" No causal claim — this is a question about the deterministic numerical behavior
of a specific, fully-specified iterative algorithm.

## The formal object (from the primary source, not paraphrased)

For SPD `A in R^{n x n}`, `b in R^n`, minimize `f(x) = (1/2) x^T A x - x^T b`. Restarted
s-step optimal-gradient iteration:
```
y_k = r_k / ||r_k||,  r_k = b - A x_k
x_{k+1} in x_k + K_s(A, y_k)  such that  x - x_{k+1} perp_A K_s(A, y_k)
```
where `K_s(A, y_k)` is the s-dimensional Krylov subspace generated by `A` and `y_k`.

**Problem 2.20 (Forsythe conjecture).** For `2 <= s < d(A)` (`d(A)` = number of distinct
eigenvalues of A), each of `{y_2k}` and `{y_2k+1}` has a single limit vector.

## The claim (falsifiable)

1. **Primary (counterexample search, s>=3, genuinely open territory):** for SPD matrices
   A of dimension n in {5, 8, 12} and restart length s in {3, ..., n-1}, drawn both (a)
   randomly (Wishart-distributed) and (b) adversarially (clustered/near-degenerate
   eigenvalue spectra, designed to stress Krylov convergence), run the restarted iteration
   for up to 500 restarts and test convergence via
   `d_k = max(||y_{2k+2} - y_{2k}||, ||y_{2k+3} - y_{2k+1}||)`.
   Kill criterion: **CONFIRMED** (conjecture holds on this sample) if `d_k -> 0` for every
   tested (A, s) pair within 500 restarts (defined as `d_k < 1e-8` sustained for the last
   20 restarts); **REJECTED** (real counterexample) if any tested pair shows `d_k` failing
   to decay below `1e-6` after 500 restarts AND the residual norm itself is not simply
   converging to zero for an unrelated reason (checked separately, see Controls below).
2. **Secondary (independent replication check, s=2):** since three informal, unreviewed
   proofs specifically for s=2 appeared in the same month as this experiment (August 2026),
   run the same convergence test at s=2 on an independent, freshly-drawn matrix sample as a
   numerical sanity check on the very recent claims — NOT a substitute for peer review, an
   independent numerical data point per the Independent Verification Strength Ladder
   (falsification-ladder.md), which this project has used before (ADR-083's own citation of
   the 2026-09-08 external-audit incident).

## Mechanism Claim Gate (Step 0a)

**Triggering sentence:** "the restarted s-step optimal-gradient iteration always produces
a well-defined `x_{k+1}` (the A-orthogonality condition has a solution) provided
`d(A, r_0) >= s+1`" (implicit in the paper's own construction, cited in its Background
section around eq. 4-5, `d(A,r_0)>=s+1` guarantees `r_1 != 0`).
**Check:** verified directly in `run.py`'s `restart_step()` — the s-dimensional Krylov
subspace is built via a short Lanczos-style recurrence starting from `y_k`; if the
recurrence produces a rank-deficient basis (breakdown), the run is flagged and excluded
from the convergence statistic rather than silently treated as convergence. **HOLDS** as
implemented (verified via the breakdown-detection test in `tests/`).

## What this does NOT mean

1. Does NOT prove or disprove the Forsythe conjecture in general — a finite numerical
   search over n<=12, s<=11, 500 restarts cannot establish a universal claim over all SPD
   matrices and all s. A CONFIRMED verdict here is numerical evidence consistent with (not
   proof of) the conjecture, matching what the source paper itself already states
   ("numerical evidence suggests the conjecture is true").
2. Does NOT constitute peer review of the three 2026 s=2 proofs — the secondary check is an
   independent numerical data point, not a verification of their mathematical arguments.
3. Does NOT explore restart lengths or dimensions beyond the tested ranges (n<=12 is a real
   computational limit for this experiment's adversarial-matrix construction, not a claim
   that larger n would behave identically).
4. A REJECTED verdict (counterexample found) would be a genuine, high-value mathematical
   finding — but per this project's calibration on prior high-difficulty theoretical
   candidates (ADR-084/085), the base rate of an amateur small-scale numerical search
   finding what 58 years of numerical linear algebra research has missed is treated as LOW
   a priori; this experiment is scoped and reported as a cheap first check, not an
   expectation of discovery.
