## 1. Verdict

### `RBA SURVIVES DECOMPOSITION TEST` -- and in the strong form the pre-registered criterion named

The pre-registered "especially strong positive signal" was: *`q*E[R1]` and
`q*E[R2]` both grow large in magnitude with opposite sign, but `q*E[R1+R2]=O(1)`
(real second-order cancellation).*  That is what was measured, and the growth is
significant in the components and absent in the sum by a factor of 15-21 in the
`t`-statistic:

| quantity | slope per unit `log q` | SE | t | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `q*E[R1]` | **-8.1422** | 0.5324 | **-15.29** | `<1e-15` |
| `q*E[R2]` | **+7.8489** | 0.3703 | **+21.19** | `<1e-15` |
| `q*E[R1+R2]` | -0.2874 | 0.3116 | -0.92 | 0.356 |
| `q*E[R_q]` | -0.3114 | 0.3062 | -1.02 | 0.309 |

The two component slopes cancel to
`|-8.1422 + 7.8489| / (8.1422 + 7.8489) = 0.0183` -- **98.2% of the logarithmic
growth cancels**, and what is left is statistically indistinguishable from zero.
`q*E[R_q]` stays in `[+0.729, +1.424]` across an 8x range of `q` (127 -> 1023),
and a constant model fits it with `chi2 = 1.054` on 3 dof (`p = 0.79`) -- i.e.
the constant hypothesis is not merely un-rejected, it is a good fit.

**The one honest qualifier, stated up front rather than buried.** The four point
estimates of `q*E[R_q]` decrease monotonically (`+1.424, +1.255, +0.994,
+0.729`), 4 out of 4.  That decrease is **not significant** (`p = 0.309`), and
the 95% CIs overlap heavily, but the data also cannot *exclude* a slow decline:
the 2-sigma bound on the slope is `|b| <~ 0.92` per unit `log q`.  A sustained
decline of that kind would mean `R = Theta(log q / q)`, not `O(1/q)`.  Four sizes
cannot settle this, and no asymptotic theorem is claimed from four sizes --
that limitation is stated explicitly regardless of outcome, as the spec required.

**Not claimed:** RBA is not proven.  What is established is that the `R1+R2`
cancellation is real, large, and of exactly the second-order character the route
needs -- not that `R = O(1/q)` holds asymptotically.

## 5. Interpretation

**Are `|q*R1|` and `|q*R2|` growing?  Yes, unambiguously, and logarithmically.**
`q*E[R1]` runs `-18.86 -> -24.35 -> -30.58 -> -35.68` and `q*E[R2]` runs
`+18.65 -> +23.99 -> +29.98 -> +34.81` over `q = 127, 255, 513, 1023`.  Each
doubling of `q` adds roughly `5.6` to the magnitude of each, which is a clean
`c*log q`: the weighted fits give `-8.14` and `+7.85` per unit `log q` with
`t = -15.3` and `+21.2`.  These are not noise-level trends; the constant model
for the components is rejected at `chi2 = 234` and `450` on 3 dof.

**Does the cancellation hold, and does it improve with `n`?  It holds; it does
NOT improve -- it degrades slowly.**  `cancel_ratio = |E[R1+R2]|/(|E[R1]|+|E[R2]|)`
runs `0.0056 -> 0.0074 -> 0.0100 -> 0.0123`, i.e. the cancellation fraction falls
from 99.44% to 98.77%.  The pre-registered expectation was `CANCEL_RATIO << 1,
possibly decreasing with n`; the first half is confirmed by a wide margin, the
second half is **not** -- the ratio rises monotonically, because the residual
`|q*(R1+R2)|` grows (`0.211 -> 0.356 -> 0.604 -> 0.864`) somewhat faster than the
components do.  Reporting this the other way round would have been the easy
misread: a ratio of ~1% is so small that its slow worsening is easy to wave
through, and it is the single most adverse trend in the dataset.

**Is there evidence of `q*R ~ log(q)`?**  Not at the level of significance, but
the data lean that way and cannot rule it out.  The two model-selection criteria
disagree, and the disagreement is structural rather than informative: AICc with
`N = 4` and `k = 2` carries a `2k(k+1)/(N-k-1) = +12` small-sample penalty that
mechanically hands the win to the 1-parameter constant (`dAICc ~ 11`), while
LOOCV on 4 points mechanically favours the flexible model (log-model RMSE
`0.0741` vs constant `0.3624`, a 4.9x difference).  Neither criterion settles it;
the slope's own significance does, and it is `p = 0.309`.  The `chi2` comparison
says the same thing in one number: adding the log term buys
`delta chi2 = 1.054 - 0.020 = 1.03` for one extra parameter.

**Does this support `RBA = O(1/q)`?**  It supports the *mechanism* strongly and
the *conclusion* weakly.  The mechanism -- that `R1` and `R2` are individually
`Theta(log q / q)` and that their sum is a genuine second-order cancellation --
is now measured, not assumed, and the identities underwriting it hold to
`1e-15`.  The conclusion `q*E[R] = O(1)` is consistent with everything measured
and is the best-fitting simple model, but over only an 8x range of `q` with a
monotone (if insignificant) downward drift.

**My own assessment: genuine second-order cancellation, not an artifact.**  Four
independent reasons, each of which an artifact would have had to survive:

1. The cancellation is not imposed by construction.  `R1` and `R2` come from
   different objects -- `R1` from `<p,u_j>` plus the exactly-computable `g_j A_j`
   bulk term, `R2` from `||u_j||^2`, which is sign-definite (`R2 > 0` always) and
   therefore cannot be tuned to match `R1`.  That `R2 > 0` grows at `+7.85` per
   `log q` while `R1 < 0` grows at `-8.14` is a fact about the LP's optimal face,
   not an algebraic tautology.
2. The two mandatory identities are independent of the cancellation and both
   pass at machine precision (`<= 1.06e-18` bulk, `<= 1.39e-15` delta-I), and a
   planted-error control shows those gates actually have power -- all 5 injected
   errors were caught at all 4 tested `n`, the smallest planted violation being
   `5.8e-08` against a `1e-9` threshold.
3. Two structurally different LP algorithms (dual simplex and interior point)
   reproduce `R_total` parent-by-parent to `<= 6.4e-07` with `0 / 80` support
   mismatches -- seven orders of magnitude below the statistic's own
   parent-to-parent SD (~4-5), so no part of the effect is solver-dependent.
4. The exhaustive small-`n` control, which uses no Monte Carlo at all, shows the
   same signature independently: over `q = 4..10`, `|q*R1|` and `|q*R2|` grow
   `4.29 -> 7.50` and `3.59 -> 7.06` while `q*R_total` stays in `[0.72, 1.24]`.

**The one thing that does not fit a single clean story, flagged rather than
smoothed over.**  Across the full range the trend in `q*R_total` is
*non-monotone*: it rises over the exhaustive small-`n` range
(`0.725 -> 1.238` for `q = 4 -> 10`), peaks somewhere near `q ~ 127`
(`+1.424`), then falls over the Monte-Carlo range (`-> +0.729` at `q = 1023`).
Neither a constant nor a pure `log q` model describes both halves.  The small-`n`
points are exact or near-exact and the large-`n` points carry wide CIs, so this
is a real shape and not a numerical artifact -- but the small-`n` central layer
(`F = 4..10` free orbits) is not obviously in the same regime as `F = 1023`, and
the two halves are deliberately NOT pooled into one fit here.

## 6. Controls

### 6a. Substrate gate (positive control on the LP itself)

The reduced frequency LP used here is a *different formulation* from the
project's own `theta_via_lp` (time-domain primal, Table 1 of arXiv:2603.29571,
imported unchanged from `20260909-lovasz-theta-random-circulant-graphs/run.py`).
They must agree via `theta(G) = n * w_0`.  Checked on primes `n = 11..47` with
both solver methods: **max `|n*w_0 - theta_via_lp| = 2.842e-14`**, and the parent
support was exactly `q` in every case.  This is an oracle-level check, not merely
an aggregation-level one: the two paths share no LP code.

### 6b. Injected-error control (do the gates have power?)

`rba_negative_control.py` plants five errors of exactly the kind that would

| n | q | F | spec target | executed | children/parent | full-j audit parents |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 509 | 127 | 127 | >=150 parents (aim 200), >=32 children | **200** | 32 | 3 |

Every target was met at the *aim* level, `n=4093` included -- no target was
silently reduced.  Two deviations, both stated rather than absorbed:

- **The `n=4093` full-j audit was not run.**  It needs `F+1 = 1024` child LPs for
  a single parent at ~45.8 s each, i.e. ~13 h of serial solve time for one audit
  point.  The subset-vs-full-j comparison therefore rests on `n = 509, 1021,
  2053` (6 audited parents), where the subset estimator tracks the exact all-`j`
  mean within ordinary subset noise (section "Sanity check 1").
- **The symmetry shortcut (fix `j=1`) was NOT used.**  It is implemented
  (`--fixed-j`) and was allowed, but proved unnecessary: switching the solver
  from dual simplex to interior point cut the `n=4093` LP from **212 s to ~37 s**
  (6x), which made the standard random-`j` protocol affordable.  No warm-start
  was needed either.  The LP *formulation* was never altered -- only the solve
  method, exactly as the spec permitted.

Wall-clock: `n=4093` stage ran 2825 s on 8 worker processes (mean parent LP
45.8 s, mean child LP 45.1 s); 450 LP solves.  The main dataset is 556 parent
LP solves + 12204 child LP solves = 12760 (the control datasets add ~9000 more).

## 9. What this result does NOT mean

1. It does **not** prove RBA.  It tests one thing: whether `R1+R2` is a genuine
   `O(1/q)` second-order cancellation.  It is, over the tested range.
2. It does **not** establish an asymptotic theorem.  Four sizes, spanning 8x in
   `q`, cannot distinguish a bounded `q*E[R]` from one drifting like `c*log q`
   with `|c| <~ 0.9`.  No asymptotic claim is made from four sizes.
3. It does **not** transfer to composite `n`.  Uniqueness of the optimum, the
   exact support size `q`, and the orthogonality identity behind the bulk
   relation all use primality of `n`.
4. It does **not** speak to `R_n` or to `Var(X_n)` directly.  `R(S)` here is the
   layer-local decomposition quantity, not the experiment's `R_n = Var(X)-W_1`.
5. The `E[D]/(E[I]-1)` ADC cross-check (`K_ADC = 1.2950, 1.2658, 1.2337,
   1.2161`) is a consistency diagnostic, not an independent confirmation -- both
   moments come from the same optimizer.

## 10. Next open question (named, not attempted)

The **CANCEL lemma**:

```
E_{S,j}[ 2( <p,u_j> + g_j A_j ) + ||u_j||^2 ] = O(q^{-2})
```

This is the analytic statement the measured cancellation would follow from.  It
was explicitly out of scope here and no attempt at it is made in this document.

**Intersection with the concurrent `codex-20260914-susceptibility/` line
(Points 63-64), noted without duplicating its machinery.**  That work decomposes
`n||y*||^2 = (n/s)(1 + CV_support^2)` for the positive optimal measure.  The
quantity `I(S) = q * sum p_k^2` measured here is *exactly* `1 + CV^2` of `p` over
its own support (since `sum p_k = 1`, `q*sum p_k^2 - 1 = Var(p)/mean(p)^2`), so
`E[I]` here is directly comparable to their `mean 1+CV^2`.  They report
`1.9802 / 1.9703 / 2.0401` at `n = 127 / 509 / 2039`; this run gives
`E[I] = 1.6348 / 1.6117 / 1.5985 / 1.5933` at `n = 509 / 1021 / 2053 / 4093`.
**These are close but NOT equal, and the gap is expected, not a contradiction**:
their support is the full `s >= 2Q+1` time-domain support of `y*` under a
randomly-sampled graph, while this layer's support is exactly the `q` frequency
orbits of a *fixed-density* central-layer parent -- different objects on
different sides of the transform.  Both stay bounded near 2, which is the
substantive point of contact.  The divergence worth flagging for whoever
reconciles them: `E[I]` here is slowly **decreasing** in `n` (1.6348 -> 1.5933),
whereas their `1+CV^2` is roughly flat-to-rising (1.9802 -> 2.0401).  No attempt
is made here to resolve that -- it is their formulation, their gate.
